# Two-Step Gated GRPO Method

Status: research-method specification. The first-stage $`A_1`$ and $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ use the same action-mean-normalized advantage. Select the lowest 20% of visit positions at an identical complete state; repeated visits by one trajectory count separately and may be selected separately. If a boundary action has too many visits, sample only the visits needed to fill the quota. Parent and child standard deviations in the $`\Delta`$ gate give equal weight to distinct action means. Add $`\lambda`$ only to selected visit positions whose action passes the downstream gate. Selected second actions $`a'`$ use $`\beta A_2`$, where $`\beta>1`$; unselected $`a'`$ use $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$. Retain the additive formula when adjacent two-step chains overlap. Broadcast each step's training advantage to all tokens generated by the model at that step, calculate the policy ratio, clipping, and KL per token, and average over tokens within each trajectory step. The Game24 implementation and CPU checks are complete; see the [Game24 protocol](./game24_experiment_protocol_and_logging.md) and [configuration](./game24_experiment/experiment.json) for settings and the INVALID special case. The actual 4B model has not been trained on an A100. Task-specific settings and theoretical limits appear in Section 7.

## 1. Motivation and confirmed design

Starting from state $`s_t`$, the first action $`a`$ may have an average terminal outcome close to the mean across actions at that state, leaving its action-level advantage weak. After reaching $`s_{t+1}=(s_t,a)`$, however, different next actions $`a'`$ may separate clearly into positive and negative outcomes. The method seeks to increase entry into such child states moderately and then distinguish good from bad $`a'`$ more strongly there.

The law of total variance motivates this design. Let $`h=(s_t,a)`$ and $`u=a'`$. Then

```math
\operatorname{Var}(R\mid h)
=\operatorname{Var}_{u\mid h}\!\bigl(\mathbb E[R\mid h,u]\bigr)
+\mathbb E_{u\mid h}\!\bigl[\operatorname{Var}(R\mid h,u)\bigr].
```

The first term describes variation among the average terminal outcomes of different $`a'`$; the second describes outcome variation within the same $`a'`$. The current method first averages terminal rewards by action at both parent and child states, then uses the equal-weight standard deviation of distinct action means for the $`\Delta`$ gate. It neither estimates nor gates the second term separately, and it does not add a branch-estimate reliability gate. Because the method weights distinct actions equally while the variance identity weights them by their action probabilities, these are not exactly the same variance.

The confirmed core rules are:

1. At $`s_t`$, average terminal rewards by action to compute $`A_{1,i,j}=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}`$ for every visit position. Select the 20% of visit positions with the smallest $`|A_{1,i,j}|`$. Visits choosing the same action have the same advantage, but only some may be selected. Retain all selected visits while listing a repeated action $`a`$ only once as a gate candidate.
2. At both the parent state and the child state reached by candidate $`a`$, first average rewards within each action; then compute $`\sigma_t,\sigma_{t+1}`$ with equal weight across distinct action means. Require $`\sigma_{t+1}\geq\omega\sigma_t`$. Zero-variance groups fail the gate.
3. Require at least five distinct observed $`a'`$ at the child state. Use the same action means to calculate second-stage advantage $`A_2`$.
4. Select $`m(d)=\lceil d/2\rceil`$ distinct $`a'`$ by largest $`|A_2(a')|`$, where $`d`$ is the number of distinct $`a'`$. Break ties at the cutoff by random sampling to fill the quota. Selected actions keep the sign of $`A_2`$ in their training coefficient; unselected actions use $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$.
5. A visit selected in the first stage receives the fixed advantage increment $`\lambda`$ only if its action $`a`$ has at least one downstream $`a'`$ passing selection. Unselected visits of the same $`a`$ receive no $`\lambda`$. Selected second actions are weighted by their signed advantages, including negative-advantage actions.
6. A visit receives $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ when it receives no first-stage $`\lambda`$ and is not the second step of a passed gate chain. An unselected $`a'`$ in a passed chain also uses $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$. When two adjacent two-step chains overlap in a long trajectory, add the two corrections according to the overlap formula.

Earlier proposals involving $`M(s_t,a)`$, a covariance-based parent-action reward, and state-mean centering of $`M`$ have been superseded and are not part of the current method. The earlier trajectory-level $`A^{\mathrm{orig}}`$ fallback has been replaced by $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$, and the first-stage $`A_1`$ uses the same action-mean algorithm. At a passed child state with exactly the same old visits, $`A^{\mathrm{state}}=A_2`$; a non-overlapping second-step advantage can therefore be written as $`(1+(\beta-1)\mathbf1_{\mathcal S})A_2`$.

## 2. Grouping old trajectories, first-stage advantage, and common fallback

Let $`R_i`$ be the terminal reward of old trajectory $`\tau_i`$. Let $`s_{i,j}`$ and $`a_{i,j}`$ be its complete state and action at decision position $`j`$. Below, $`s_t`$ denotes one state being analyzed; trajectories need not reach it at the same step index. For that state, define

```math
\mathcal I_t(s_t)=\{(i,j):s_{i,j}=s_t\},
\qquad K_t=|\mathcal I_t(s_t)|.
```

Counting by visit position is confirmed: If one trajectory reaches exactly the same state at different positions $`j`$, each $`(i,j)`$ belongs to $`\mathcal I_t`$. Those visits may share $`R_i`$, but they are separate actual decisions and must not be treated as statistically independent trajectories. The action-mean statistics and first-stage 20% selection below use these visit positions as the counting unit.

Following the general definition in Section 2.1, average terminal rewards within each action to obtain $`q_{s_t}(u)`$, then give distinct action means equal weight when calculating $`\bar q_{s_t}`$ and the standard deviation. With $`d_t=|\mathcal U(s_t)|`$, denote this standard deviation at the parent state by $`\sigma_t`$:

```math
\sigma_t:=\sigma_{q,s_t}
=\sqrt{\frac{1}{d_t}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U(s_t)}
\bigl(q_{s_t}(u)-\bar q_{s_t}\bigr)^2}.
```

Use these same statistics to calculate first-stage advantage and assign it to each visit position:

```math
A_{1,i,j}:=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}
=\frac{q_{s_t}(a_{i,j})-\bar q_{s_t}}{\sigma_t},
\qquad (i,j)\in\mathcal I_t(s_t).
```

This formula is used for first-stage selection only when $`\sigma_t>0`$. If $`\sigma_t=0`$, the state supplies no first-stage candidates; as a fallback, its visit advantage remains $`A^{\mathrm{state}}=0`$. $`K_t`$ is the number of visits to the state and determines the 20% quota; $`d_t`$ is the number of distinct actions and determines the equal-weight standard deviation.

Interpret "fill 20%, or exceed it slightly" as the smallest integer quota meeting that fraction:

```math
M_t=\left\lceil 0.2K_t\right\rceil .
```

Select exactly $`M_t`$ visit positions with the smallest $`|A_{1,i,j}|`$, and denote them by $`\mathcal L_t(s_t)`$. The candidate first actions are

```math
\mathcal C_t(s_t)=\{a_{i,j}:(i,j)\in\mathcal L_t(s_t)\}.
```

Selection acts on visit positions first; candidate actions are deduplicated afterward. All visits choosing the same $`a`$ have the same $`A_1`$. If $`r<M_t`$ positions have been selected and adding every visit of boundary action $`a`$ would exceed $`M_t`$, sample only $`M_t-r`$ of its unselected visits uniformly without replacement. Do not force the entire action group into the quota. If different actions also tie at the boundary $`|A_1|`$, sample the remaining positions from the pooled tied visits. Thus, the same $`a`$ may have both selected and unselected visits, and repeated visits by one trajectory may also differ. List $`a`$ as a candidate if at least one of its visits is selected.

Equivalently, let $`c_t`$ be the $`M_t`$-th smallest $`|A_{1,i,j}|`$. Include all visits with $`|A_{1,i,j}|<c_t`$, then sample among visits with $`|A_{1,i,j}|=c_t`$ until the total is $`M_t`$. Since $`M_t=\lceil0.2K_t\rceil`$, the selected fraction is exactly 20% when $`0.2K_t`$ is an integer; otherwise the count exceeds $`0.2K_t`$ by less than one visit. For example, if $`K_t=23`$, then $`M_t=5`$. If three visits already have smaller $`|A_1|`$ and boundary action $`a`$ has four visits, sample two of those four.

In particular, if several selected visits choose the same $`a`$, that action is still evaluated only once as a gate candidate, while all those selected visits remain. Record separately

```math
m_t(a)=|\{(i,j)\in\mathcal L_t:a_{i,j}=a\}|,\qquad
n_t(a)=|\{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_t:a_{i,j}=a\}|,
```

Here $`m_t(a)`$ is the number of first-stage selected visits of $`a`$, and $`n_t(a)`$ is the number of all old visits choosing $`a`$ at this state. If the selected 20% actions are $`[a,a,a,b]`$, then the candidate set is $`\{a,b\}`$, with $`m_t(a)=3`$ and $`m_t(b)=1`$. Evaluate the gate for $`a`$ once. If it passes, each of the three selected visits of $`a`$ gets one $`\lambda`$, not three $`\lambda`$ at one visit. Unselected visits of the same action receive none. Child-state statistics use all old visits to that child, not merely first-stage selected visits.

### 2.1 Action-mean advantage $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ at any state

At any decision state $`s`$, collect all old visits reaching it. Let $`\mathcal I(s)=\{(i,j):s_{i,j}=s\}`$, $`\mathcal U(s)=\{a_{i,j}:(i,j)\in\mathcal I(s)\}`$, and $`d_s=|\mathcal U(s)|`$. For each distinct next action $`u\in\mathcal U(s)`$, average terminal rewards within its visits:

```math
\mathcal I(s,u)=\{(i,j)\in\mathcal I(s):a_{i,j}=u\},\qquad
n_s(u)=|\mathcal I(s,u)|,\qquad
q_s(u)=\frac{1}{n_s(u)}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I(s,u)}R_i.
```

Then give distinct action means equal weight when computing

```math
\bar q_s=\frac{1}{d_s}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U(s)}q_s(u),\qquad
\sigma_{q,s}=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{d_s}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U(s)}(q_s(u)-\bar q_s)^2}.
```

Assign the normalized action advantage to every old visit choosing that action at the state:

```math
\boxed{
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{q_s(a_{i,j})-\bar q_s}{\sigma_{q,s}},&\sigma_{q,s}>0,\\[6pt]
0,&\sigma_{q,s}=0.
\end{cases}
}
\qquad (i,j)\in\mathcal I(s).
```

Thus visits at the same state choosing the same next action share $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$, even if their trajectories have different terminal rewards. If there is only one distinct action, or all distinct action means are equal, then $`\sigma_{q,s}=0`$ and the fallback advantage for each visit is zero; the entire trajectory is not discarded. Action-mean normalization does not weight by action frequency. When the GRPO objective is later averaged over trajectories and positions, an action appearing $`n_s(u)`$ times still contributes $`n_s(u)`$ position terms. Training uses no additional $`1/n_s(u)`$ factor or other repeat-count correction. If one trajectory revisits $`s`$, its $`R_i`$ is counted once per relevant visit; these records are not statistically independent trajectories.

$`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ is the new definition replacing the earlier $`A^{\mathrm{orig}}`$ in this method; it is not the trajectory-level advantage of original outcome GRPO. First-stage $`A_{1,i,j}`$ uses the same algorithm and selects the 20% with smallest $`|A_1|`$ by visit position. See the [original outcome GRPO advantage definition](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03300).

## 3. Second stage: the $`\Delta`$ gate, action means, and absolute-advantage selection

### 3.1 Old-trajectory visits at the child state

For each $`a\in\mathcal C_t(s_t)`$, let $`s_{t+1}=(s_t,a)`$. Use all old visits reaching that child state, rather than only visits selected in the first stage:

```math
\mathcal I_{t+1}(s_{t+1})
=\{(i,j):s_{i,j}=s_{t+1}\},
\qquad K_{t+1}=|\mathcal I_{t+1}(s_{t+1})|.
```

Here $`K_{t+1}`$ counts child-state visits. Section 3.2 computes the child-state standard deviation by giving distinct $`a'`$ reward means equal weight, not by taking the standard deviation of the terminal rewards attached directly to these $`K_{t+1}`$ visits.

### 3.2 Average within each $`a'`$, then normalize across actions

Let $`\mathcal U_{t+1}`$ be the distinct next actions actually observed at this child state, with $`d=|\mathcal U_{t+1}|`$. If $`d<5`$, reject candidate $`a`$. For each $`u\in\mathcal U_{t+1}`$, define

```math
\mathcal I_{t+1,u}
=\{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_{t+1}:a_{i,j}=u\},
\qquad
n_u=|\mathcal I_{t+1,u}|,
\qquad
q(u)=\frac{1}{n_u}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I_{t+1,u}}R_i.
```

Calculate with equal weight across distinct action means:

```math
\bar q=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{t+1}}q(u),
\qquad
\sigma_q=
\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{u\in\mathcal U_{t+1}}(q(u)-\bar q)^2}.
```

Here $`q(u)=q_{s_{t+1}}(u)`$ and $`\bar q=\bar q_{s_{t+1}}`$, so this standard deviation is the value of the general formula in Section 2.1 at child state $`s_{t+1}`$. The three names used for the $`\Delta`$ gate denote the same quantity:

```math
\boxed{\sigma_{t+1}:=\sigma_q=\sigma_{q,s_{t+1}}.}
```

If $`\sigma_{t+1}=0`$, candidate $`a`$ fails the downstream gate. For the remaining candidates, check

```math
G_\Delta(s_t,a)=
\mathbf1\{\sigma_{t+1}\geq\omega\sigma_t\}
=\mathbf1\{\sigma_q\geq\omega\sigma_{q,s_t}\}.
```

$`\omega`$ is a task-specific constant; the first Game24 run fixes it at $`1`$, with no parameter search. This gate directly compares the dispersion of distinct action-mean terminal rewards at the parent and child states. Within-action variation in terminal rewards does not directly raise $`\sigma_{t+1}`$. If $`\sigma_{t+1}>0`$ and the $`\Delta`$ gate passes, define second-stage action advantage

```math
A_2(s_{t+1},u)=\frac{q(u)-\bar q}{\sigma_q}.
```

Old visits choosing the same $`a'`$ receive the same $`A_2`$. Because $`\sigma_q^2`$ gives actions equal weight, it is generally not equal to the first term of the law of total variance, which weights by old-policy action probabilities.

### 3.3 The second-action set and final first-stage gate

Once all preceding checks pass, the second-stage quota is fixed as

```math
m(d)=\left\lceil\frac d2\right\rceil .
```

Select exactly $`m(d)`$ distinct actions $`u`$ with the largest $`|A_2(s_{t+1},u)|`$, denoting the set by $`\mathcal S(s_t,a)`$. For example, select three when $`d=5`$. At a tie across the cutoff, keep actions strictly above the cutoff and sample the rest uniformly without replacement from the tied actions until the quota is met. If any prerequisite fails, define $`\mathcal S(s_t,a)=\varnothing`$.

First define the candidate action's downstream gate:

```math
\boxed{
G_t^{\mathrm{act}}(s_t,a)=
\mathbf1\{\sigma_t>0\}\,
\mathbf1\{a\in\mathcal C_t(s_t)\}\,
\mathbf1\{\mathcal S(s_t,a)\neq\varnothing\}.
}
```

The first-stage visit-level reward gate adds membership in the selected 20%:

```math
\boxed{
B_{i,t}=
\mathbf1\{(i,t)\in\mathcal L_t(s_t)\}\,
G_t^{\mathrm{act}}(s_t,a_{i,t}).
}
```

Consequently, $`B_{i,t}=1\Rightarrow\mathcal S(s_t,a_{i,t})\neq\varnothing`$: if no $`a'`$ passes second-stage selection, the visit cannot receive $`\lambda`$. An unselected visit of the same action with $`(i,t)\notin\mathcal L_t`$ also receives no $`\lambda`$. If a trajectory revisits $`s_t`$, its two visit positions can have different $`B_{i,t}`$. Selection by $`|A_2|`$ may include negative-advantage $`a'`$; there is no additional requirement that both signs be represented.

## 4. Advantage assignment and policy-update signal

### 4.1 First action $`a`$

For action $`a_{i,t}`$ chosen by trajectory $`i`$ at $`s_t`$, define the additional first-stage increment precisely as

```math
\boxed{
\Delta A^{(1)}_{i,t}
=\lambda B_{i,t}
=\lambda\,\mathbf1\{(i,t)\in\mathcal L_t(s_t)\}\,
G_t^{\mathrm{act}}(s_t,a_{i,t}),
\qquad \lambda>0.
}
```

When $`B_{i,t}=1`$, the training advantage is $`\widetilde A_{i,t}=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t}+\lambda`$, where $`A_{1,i,t}\equiv A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t}`$. Each selected visit of the same $`a`$ receives one $`\lambda`$; unselected visits of that $`a`$ receive zero increment and use fallback $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$. Even though they share the same base advantage at $`(s_t,a)`$, random tie-breaking may add $`\lambda`$ to only some of them. This samples the additional signal by visit position. The number of extra training terms for $`a`$ therefore depends on $`m_t(a)`$, not directly on its total occurrence count $`n_t(a)`$.

### 4.2 Second action $`a'`$

At a child state whose gate passes, selected $`a'`$ use amplification coefficient $`\beta`$ on their action-level advantage; unselected actions use fallback state-action advantage $`A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t+1}`$:

```math
\boxed{
\widetilde A_{i,t+1}
=
\begin{cases}
\beta A_2(s_{t+1},a_{i,t+1}),
&a_{i,t+1}\in\mathcal S(s_t,a_{i,t}),\\[2pt]
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t+1},
&a_{i,t+1}\notin\mathcal S(s_t,a_{i,t}),
\end{cases}
\qquad \beta>1.
}
```

Selected positive-advantage actions receive a larger positive coefficient; selected negative-advantage actions receive a larger-magnitude negative coefficient. Unselected actions still train with their action-mean advantage. Both $`A_2`$ and $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ use all old visits through this child state. In a passed child state, the coefficient is $`1`$ for unselected actions and $`\beta>1`$ for selected actions, increasing the selected actions' advantage magnitudes. The first-stage $`\lambda`$ is not directly broadcast to the second action. The first Game24 run fixes $`\lambda=0.5,\beta=1.5`$.

This adjustment changes the training advantage calculation, not the KL term or position averaging in the clipped GRPO objective below. If a position is also the first action of the next two-step chain, use the confirmed overlap formula in Section 4.3.

#### Relationship between $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ and $`A_2`$ at a passed child state

At the same child state $`s_{t+1}`$, if both use exactly the same old visits and next-action partition, then $`q_s,\bar q_s,\sigma_{q,s}`$ from Section 2.1 are respectively $`q,\bar q,\sigma_q`$ from Section 3.2. Since a passed gate requires $`\sigma_q>0`$, we have

```math
\boxed{
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t+1}
=A_2(s_{t+1},a_{i,t+1}).
}
```

Therefore, at a non-overlapping second step, the training advantage is equivalently

```math
\widetilde A_{i,t+1}
=\bigl[1+(\beta-1)\mathbf1\{a_{i,t+1}\in\mathcal S(s_t,a_{i,t})\}\bigr]
A_2(s_{t+1},a_{i,t+1}).
```

The coefficient is $`1`$ for unselected actions and $`\beta`$ for selected actions. This differs from the old fallback $`A^{\mathrm{orig}}`$, which normalized each trajectory's terminal reward: under that old rule, successful and failed trajectories choosing the same action could receive opposite signals. They now share a signal derived from the action's mean terminal reward.

### 4.3 Visits without a first-stage increment and overlapping visits

A visit that receives no first-stage $`\lambda`$ and is not the second step of a passed gate chain uses $`A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}`$. A non-overlapping second step in a passed chain that is not selected into $`\mathcal S`$ also uses $`A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}`$. If an action is both the previous chain's $`a'`$ and a selected visit of $`a`$ in the next chain, add the two corrections under the confirmed rule; this overlap role takes precedence and uses

```math
\widehat A_{i,j}^{\mathrm{overlap}}
=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}+\lambda
+\beta\,\mathbf1\{a_{i,j}\in\mathcal S(s_{i,j-1},a_{i,j-1})\}
A_2(s_{i,j},a_{i,j}).
```

This formula retains $`A^{\mathrm{state}}+\lambda`$ for the current position's role as the first action of the next chain, then adds the previous chain's second-stage selection signal. If the action is not in the previous chain's $`\mathcal S`$, that chain's extra action-level term is zero, but the current position still receives $`A^{\mathrm{state}}+\lambda`$ from the next chain. Thus the standalone $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ fallback applies only when $`B_{i,j}=0`$ at a non-overlapping position. Because $`A_{1,i,j}=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}=A_2(s_{i,j},a_{i,j})`$ when the preceding chain passes, the retained overlap formula means that the action-level component totals $`(1+\beta)A_2`$ if the position is selected in the preceding $`\mathcal S`$, and $`A_2`$ otherwise. This follows from adding the two roles; no other training term is introduced.

## 5. GRPO objective: what is optimized?

Let $`\pi_{\mathrm{old}}`$ be the policy that sampled the old trajectories, $`\pi_\theta`$ the policy being updated, and $`a_{i,j}`$ the action at decision position $`j`$ in trajectory $`i`$. The old policy $`\pi_{\mathrm{old}}`$ is known, so conditional probabilities of model-generated tokens in old trajectories can be computed; the denominator of the policy ratio need not be estimated.

Confirmed token-assignment rule: One edit or tool call is one decision step $`j`$. Use Section 4 and the complete position-level rule below to calculate the step's training advantage $`\widehat A_{i,j}`$ from old trajectories' terminal rewards; this is not an immediate reward supplied by the environment at each step. If the model generated $`L_{i,j}`$ tokens at that step, every model-generated token, including its reasoning text and edit or tool-call content, receives the same advantage:

```math
\widehat A_{i,j,k}=\widehat A_{i,j},\qquad k=1,\ldots,L_{i,j}.
```

Tool results, environment observations, and prompts are not tokens generated by the policy at that step, so they are not prediction targets carrying its advantage; they remain context affecting token probabilities. It is confirmed that each model-generated token gets its own conditional new-to-old policy ratio and its own clipping. Let $`h_{i,j,k}`$ be the full context visible before token $`k`$, including the current state and tokens already generated at that step, and let $`y_{i,j,k}`$ be the token. Then

```math
r_{i,j,k}(\theta)=
\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})}
{\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})},
\qquad
\ell_{\mathrm{clip}}(r,A)=
\min\{rA,\operatorname{clip}(r,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A\}.
```

For each step of each trajectory, first average the clipped and KL terms over the $`L_{i,j}`$ tokens actually generated at that step, then average over steps and trajectories as specified. Per-token KL is enabled. Let $`D^{\mathrm{KL}}_{i,j,k}(\theta)`$ be the token KL estimator defined below, with coefficient $`\kappa>0`$. The objective is

```math
\boxed{
J(\theta)=
\mathbb E_{\{\tau_i\}\sim\pi_{\mathrm{old}}}
\left[
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{T_i}\sum_{j=1}^{T_i}
\frac{1}{L_{i,j}}\sum_{k=1}^{L_{i,j}}
\left(
\ell_{\mathrm{clip}}(r_{i,j,k}(\theta),\widehat A_{i,j})
-\kappa D^{\mathrm{KL}}_{i,j,k}(\theta)
\right)
\right].
}
```

Repeated actions are weighted by the trajectory-and-position objective above: if a state-action pair occurs at $`n_s(u)`$ visits, it contributes $`n_s(u)`$ position terms; repeated visits by one trajectory also remain separate terms. Each term still carries its trajectory's $`1/(NT_i)`$ coefficient, and each model-generated token within the position carries $`1/L_{i,j}`$. No additional correction equalizes total gradient weight across actions by occurrence count; equal action weighting is used only to normalize $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ and $`A_2`$. Token averaging within a step prevents the clipped policy term from gaining more total weight merely because that step generated more tokens. This is a confirmed objective choice and differs from an entire-action policy gradient without length normalization.

Training maximizes $`J(\theta)`$, or minimizes $`-J(\theta)`$. Here $`\epsilon`$ is the clipping width and $`\kappa>0`$ is the KL coefficient; neither is the second-stage amplification coefficient $`\beta`$. KL is confirmed and computed per token. The first Game24 run uses $`\kappa=0.01`$, $`\epsilon=0.2`$, and the frozen initial model as reference policy. See the [original GRPO objective and per-token KL estimator in DeepSeekMath](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03300).

Use the same form of per-token KL estimator as original GRPO. $`\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}`$ is the reference policy; both clipped policy and KL terms are computed only on model-generated tokens and use the same within-step token average:

```math
D^{\mathrm{KL}}_{i,j,k}(\theta)=
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})}
{\pi_\theta(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})}
-\log
\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})}
{\pi_\theta(y_{i,j,k}\mid h_{i,j,k})}
-1.
```

Let $`P_{i,j}=G_{j-1}^{\mathrm{act}}(s_{i,j-1},a_{i,j-1})`$ indicate whether the preceding action passed its downstream gate. Let $`B_{i,j}`$ be the current visit-level first-stage reward gate, and abbreviate $`I_{i,j}^{\mathcal S}=\mathbf1\{a_{i,j}\in\mathcal S(s_{i,j-1},a_{i,j-1})\}`$. Section 2 equates first-stage $`A_{1,i,j}`$ with $`A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}`$, so the training formulas below use the latter directly. The complete position-level advantage rule, including overlaps of adjacent chains, is

```math
\widehat A_{i,j}=
\begin{cases}
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j},
&P_{i,j}=0,\;B_{i,j}=0,\\[2pt]
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}+\lambda,
&P_{i,j}=0,\;B_{i,j}=1,\\[2pt]
\beta A_2(s_{i,j},a_{i,j}),
&P_{i,j}=1,\;B_{i,j}=0,\;I_{i,j}^{\mathcal S}=1,\\[2pt]
A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j},
&P_{i,j}=1,\;B_{i,j}=0,\;I_{i,j}^{\mathcal S}=0,\\[2pt]
\widehat A_{i,j}^{\mathrm{overlap}},
&P_{i,j}=1,\;B_{i,j}=1.
\end{cases}
```

At a boundary position, treat the nonexistent preceding gate as zero. A current visit outside its state's selected 20% can nevertheless have $`P_{i,j}=1,B_{i,j}=0`$ if the previous action's child state passed the action-level gate; this does not add $`\lambda`$ retroactively to that preceding visit. When the fallback state's action means have zero variance, its $`A^{\mathrm{state}}=0`$. Gates, selected sets, terminal-reward statistics, and advantages are precomputed from old trajectories and held fixed for one GRPO update; gradients flow only through current-policy per-token ratios and per-token KL terms. These cases complete the algebraic position-level rule; formal experimental settings remain in Section 7.

## 6. Theoretical computation procedure

1. Collect old trajectories and terminal rewards. At each identical complete state $`s`$, gather all old visits, counting repeated visits by one trajectory separately. Average terminal rewards within each next-action group, normalize with equal weight across distinct action means, and assign $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ to each visit. If action-mean variance is zero, its fallback advantage is zero.
2. At the parent state, average terminal rewards by next action and give distinct action means equal weight to compute $`\sigma_t=\sigma_{q,s_t}`$ and $`A_{1,i,j}=A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,j}`$. If $`\sigma_t=0`$, that state yields no first-stage gate candidates; its fallback advantage is $`A^{\mathrm{state}}=0`$, while per-token KL is still computed.
3. Set the first-stage visit quota to $`M_t=\lceil0.2K_t\rceil`$ and select exactly $`M_t`$ positions by smallest $`|A_{1,i,j}|`$. If all visits of a boundary action $`a`$ exceed the quota, randomly sample only enough of them to fill it. If multiple actions tie at the boundary, sample from the pooled tied positions. List a repeated first action only once as candidate $`a`$, but retain all selected positions; unselected visits of the same action receive no $`\lambda`$.
4. For each candidate $`a`$, use all old visits through its child state. Require at least five distinct observed $`a'`$. Average rewards within each $`a'`$ to obtain $`q(a')`$, then give distinct action means equal weight to compute $`\sigma_{t+1}=\sigma_q`$. A zero-variance candidate fails the gate.
5. Check the $`\Delta`$ gate $`\sigma_{t+1}\geq\omega\sigma_t`$. If it passes, calculate signed $`A_2(a')`$ using the same $`q(a'),\bar q,\sigma_q`$; do not compute another standard deviation of individual trajectory rewards.
6. Select $`m(d)=\lceil d/2\rceil`$ distinct $`a'`$ with the largest $`|A_2|`$, randomly filling ties at the cutoff. If the set is empty, first action $`a`$ cannot pass the gate.
7. Only a visit with $`B_{i,t}=1`$ uses $`A^{\mathrm{state}}_{i,t}+\lambda`$ at the first step. Unselected visits of the same action receive no $`\lambda`$ and use $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ when non-overlapping. At a second-step position that is not also the first step of the next chain, use $`\beta A_2(a')`$ for $`a'\in\mathcal S`$, and $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ otherwise; at a passed child state the latter is exactly the unscaled $`A_2(a')`$. Use Section 4.3 for overlaps. Other positions also use $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$.
8. Assign each position's advantage $`\widehat A`$ to all model-generated tokens of that step. Compute the new-to-old policy ratio and clipping separately for each token, alongside per-token KL. Average over tokens within each trajectory step, then over steps and trajectories. For overlap positions, calculate the step advantage once under the confirmed additive formula in Section 4.3. Remaining formal experimental parameters are in Section 7; do not start training that depends on any still-undetermined choices.

## 7. Task-specific settings and theoretical limitations

The following choices change the meaning of the method. Game24 fixed them in its dedicated protocol; they must not be silently carried over as defaults to a new task:

- Token-update rule and KL. All model-generated tokens of a step share its advantage. Each token has its own policy ratio and clipping, plus a per-token KL term. Average both terms over tokens within each step, then over steps and trajectories. Content not generated by the model is context only, not a prediction target carrying the step advantage. The two-step method and single-step baseline use the same KL coefficient and reference policy.
- Parameters and formal experiment. The first Game24 run uses $`\omega=1,\lambda=0.5,\beta=1.5,\epsilon=0.2,\kappa=0.01`$. Its reference policy, data split, budget, failure handling, stopping condition, and evaluation rules are in the dedicated protocol. These are authorized experimental choices, not proven optimal values. No formal experiment has been completed.

Keep the following limitations in mind when interpreting results:

- The first-stage "near zero" quantity is now an action-mean advantage, and the quota counts visit positions. Ties among visits of one action at the cutoff may select only some, so the number receiving $`\lambda`$ also depends on the action's frequency in old data and random sampling; repeated visits by one trajectory may likewise differ. With only two distinct actions and unequal action means, their absolute equal-weight-normalized advantages necessarily tie, so the 20% selection cannot distinguish them by $`|A_1|`$. Zero action-mean variance yields no first-stage candidates.
- If all parent-state action means are equal, even though individual trajectories within actions may have both success and failure, the new $`\sigma_t=0`$ and the state yields no first-stage candidates. This follows directly from using variance across action means and the confirmed zero-variance rule.
- $`M_t=\lceil0.2K_t\rceil`$ exceeds $`0.2K_t`$ by less than one visit, but when $`K_t`$ is small the selected fraction can be substantially above 20%. The current method has no additional minimum first-stage $`K_t`$.
- $`A^{\mathrm{state}}`$ gives visits with the same state and action one shared signal, hiding differences in their trajectories' terminal rewards at that action position. Later positions can still compute new advantages at their own states. This is a direct consequence of the action-mean design, not proof that all continuations of that action are equally good or bad.
- $`\sigma_{t+1}=\sigma_q`$ measures dispersion only among the mean outcomes of distinct observed $`a'`$; variation in terminal rewards within an $`a'`$ does not directly increase it. However, a rare branch's $`q(a')`$ may be poorly estimated. The current method does not add a reliability gate. Parent and child states may also have different numbers of observed actions; experiments should check the stability of comparing their equal-weight standard deviations.
- Selecting the top $`m(d)=\lceil d/2\rceil`$ distinct actions by absolute advantage may omit one sign entirely, or even select an action with $`A_2=0`$. There is no additional positive/negative quota or minimum absolute-advantage threshold.
- After the first action receives $`\lambda`$, its child state may become more likely. Even if the conditional probability of negative-advantage $`a'`$ falls, the complete bad-chain probability $`\pi(a\mid s_t)\pi(a'\mid s_{t+1})`$ need not fall. This requires separate experimental examination.
