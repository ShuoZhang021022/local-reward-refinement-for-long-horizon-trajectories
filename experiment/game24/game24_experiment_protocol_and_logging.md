## 1. Confirmed task and method choices

- Model-visible state: Each step shows only the numbers currently available. The model sees neither previous operations, the original puzzle, nor other history. The current display assigns slot numbers anew at every step; equal-valued instances retain separate slot IDs within that display. This was explicitly chosen by the user.
- Action and interaction: One model call submits one action selecting two current numbers and one operator. The environment executes that action before the next decision. The first version generates no free-form reasoning text. A complete answer generated in one call cannot be split into three steps.
- Action equivalence: Operand order is merged for addition and multiplication within the same pair of slots. Equal-valued instances in different slots count as different actions. Operand order matters for subtraction and division. Different operators remain different actions even if they produce the same successor number multiset. The user's latest correction replaced the earlier proposal to merge repeated instances. An action key uses the operator and selected current slot IDs, rather than only values or successor state.
- Numeric domain: Negative numbers, zero, and exact fractions are allowed as intermediate results. Division by zero is illegal. Environment calculations and success checks use exact rational numbers.
- Invalid model actions: An invalid generated action terminates its trajectory immediately with terminal reward 0. Syntax errors, nonexistent slots, division by zero, and an output containing multiple operations must have distinct error categories. A model-service failure is not an invalid model action.
- Invalid-action statistics: All invalid model outputs at a state form one `INVALID` action for action-mean and standard-deviation calculations. The five-next-action threshold counts legal actions only, and second-stage selection by absolute advantage considers only observed legal actions. `INVALID` has no valid successor state, cannot be a gated parent action, and cannot receive a `β` correction. These are confirmed Game24-specific rules.
- Model and resources: The initial model is `Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507`, with LoRA updates on one A100 80GB. The concrete LoRA scope and training budget are authorized first-run choices in Section 3; GPU memory use still needs external measurement. A formal run must pin and record the model and tokenizer revisions. The official model card describes this as a non-thinking 4B model.
- Preliminary analyses: Skip exhaustive structural analysis and frozen-model sampling diagnostics for this round, as the user clarified. Prepare the main training comparison directly.
- Main comparison: Compare the single-step action-mean baseline with the full two-step gated method. Section 5 specifies their shared settings.
- Ablations: Run only those two arms in this round. Lambda-only, beta-only, and random or ungated comparisons remain future proposals, not hidden first-run arms.
- Data split: Reserve the 100 official ToT puzzles at zero-based indices `[900,1000)` for testing. Stratify the other 1,262 puzzles by original difficulty order into 126 validation and 1,136 training puzzles. The same number multiset cannot cross splits. The user confirmed the principle; the assistant chose a fixed hash to select 10 validation puzzles per 100-puzzle stratum and 6 from the final 62-puzzle stratum, with seed `20260926`. `prepare` saves full puzzle ID lists and data hashes. `4 5 6 10` is in the test segment and is not used for training or as a prompt example.
- Method core: The shared rules are equal-weight normalization of action means for `A^state`, first-stage selection of 20% of visit positions, at least five observed legal child actions, the `σ` gate, `λ/β` corrections, the overlap formula, and per-token GRPO/KL. The two method documents define these rules, subject to the Game24 `INVALID` exception. The assistant selected first-run hyperparameters under the user's authorization.

Present current numbers in a fixed order. The state key is the multiset of current numbers, such as `{5,6,6}`. A model-visible display may be `[1:5, 2:6, 3:6]`: `1×2` and `1×3` are distinct actions, while `1×2` and `2×1` are the same action. Because the model sees no history, the same multiset is the same visible decision state in this interaction protocol. Slot numbers identify positions in the current fixed-order display; they do not track original numbers or earlier expression trees. Keep the prompt template, visible fields, and numbering rule consistent, and log the actual prompt and model input for each decision to check that no history leaks. The environment may retain an expression tree internally to verify that the original four numbers are each used once, without showing it to the model.

This action-equivalence rule can make distinct actions reach the same visible successor state. In the example, both multiplication actions reach `{6,30}`. When the model sees only current numbers and follows the same continuation policy, their true downstream success rates should be the same. Different finite-sample mean estimates alone do not establish meaningful second-step differentiation. Logs should identify groups of distinct actions with the same successor state and their sample counts, to help interpret the five-action threshold and standard deviations. Continue to count those actions separately as chosen by the user; do not silently merge them or add a reliability gate.

## 3. Authorized first-run configuration

- Action equivalence: Keep equal-valued instances in different slots separate. Merge operand swaps for addition or multiplication only within one slot pair; retain operand direction for subtraction or division. Keep different operators separate even when their successor states match. Apply these confirmed rules to parsing, `d_s` counts, and logs.
- Legal operations: Allow negative numbers, zero, and exact fractions; reject division by zero. Starting with four numbers, perform three legal binary operations in sequence. Success requires the sole remaining number after the third operation to be 24. Do not add solvability-based early stopping.
- Model output: Generate exactly one `left-slot right-slot operator` call per step, without free-form reasoning. Allow at most 32 new tokens. The model must emit any token in the pinned checkpoint's complete EOS set. The current official configuration uses IDs `151645/151643`; check the pinned values at run time. Include the emitted EOS in the training target and exclude padding after EOS. Exhausting the token limit without EOS is a `generation_limit` invalid output with terminal reward 0. All actually generated tokens share the step advantage.
- Invalid actions and failures: A model-generated invalid action terminates the trajectory with reward 0. Record syntax, slot, division-by-zero, and generation-length errors separately, then group them as `INVALID` for statistics. A model-service or hardware failure fails the run; it is neither relabeled as a model action nor automatically retried or skipped.
- `INVALID` in second-stage selection: Include `INVALID` in same-state action means and standard deviations, hence in `A^state/A_2`. Exclude it from the threshold of five legal child actions and from selection by `|A_2|`. In `m(d)=ceil(d/2)`, `d` counts observed legal actions. This user-confirmed Game24 exception differs from the general method documents, which describe actions without this error category.
- Model and resources: Use `Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507`, pinning the model revision during preparation, on one A100 80GB. Target LoRA modules `q/k/v/o/gate/up/down_proj`, with rank 16, alpha 32, and dropout 0. External GPU memory use and speed remain unverified. See the [official model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507).
- Sampling and training: Sample 256 trajectories per puzzle and 8 puzzles per update. Traverse the training puzzles exactly once and update once per batch of old trajectories. Use sampling temperature 1, top-p 1, top-k 0; generation batch 16, scoring batch 16, and gradient microbatch 8. Use three training seeds: `20260926/27/28`. Use AdamW, learning rate `1e-5`, weight decay 0, no gradient clipping, and no automatic early stopping.
- Method parameters: Set `ω=1.0`, `λ=0.5`, `β=1.5`, per-token clipping `ε=0.2`, and per-token KL `κ=0.01`. Both arms use the frozen initial model, with the LoRA adapter disabled, as their common reference policy. They share these settings and objective reductions. The assistant selected these values under authorization; they are not prescribed by a paper or original code.
- Evaluation: For validation and test, greedily generate one complete trajectory per puzzle. Validate before training, every 10 updates, and at the end. Select the checkpoint with highest validation success rate, breaking ties in favor of the earlier checkpoint. Test the selected checkpoint once on the official 100-puzzle set. Save per-puzzle results and call/token budgets.
- Split construction: Outside the official test segment, split the original order into 100-puzzle strata, selecting 10 validation puzzles per stratum by a fixed SHA256 ordering and 6 from the final 62. Seed `20260926` yields 1,136 training, 126 validation, and 100 test puzzles. `prepare` writes the complete lists.
- Diagnostic phases: Structural enumeration and frozen-model sampling diagnostics are postponed. Keep logs from training to check and interpret gating.

## 4. Records to retain

Use versioned structured logs. The fields below preserve enough information to recompute every `q_s(a)`, gate, advantage, and final metric. They do not add selection, caching, or training rules. Label every count with its denominator and counting unit: puzzle, trajectory, state, visit position, action, or token. Save the seed and selected IDs for random tie breaks. These logs arise directly from formal training and evaluation, without an extra preliminary experiment.

- Run: Unique run ID, time, code version and marker for uncommitted changes, configuration file and final effective parameters, data checksums, training/validation/test puzzle IDs, model and tokenizer revisions, initial/old/reference policy IDs, prompt-template version, environment and validator versions, random seeds, machine and software versions, and experiment arm. Separately record changes of old policy and checkpoint at each update.
- Puzzle: Original four numbers and canonical multiset, split, source index, attempt IDs, success or failure for each attempt, failure category, and model-call and token counts.
- Trajectory: Puzzle ID, policy version, sampling settings, trajectory ID, ordered step-record IDs, termination reason, terminal reward, final value, expression reconstructed by the environment and its validation result, numbers of legal and invalid actions, actual model calls, generated tokens, and elapsed time. Keep timeout or service failures separate from invalid model outputs.
- Decision: Position, actual model-visible state multiset and current slot IDs, actual input prompt or lossless reference, raw model output, generated token IDs/text and target mask, parse result, canonical action and equivalence key, selected slot IDs and values, operator, environment result, successor state, validity and error category, tool response, stop reason, and old-policy log probability per token. Mark prompts and tool observations not generated by the model as non-targets.
- State statistics (`state_stats`): State key, visit-position IDs, visit count `K_s`, distinct legal-action count, distinct observed legal-action count, and distinct statistical-action count including observed `INVALID`. For each action, record visit count `n_s(a)`, linked success/failure counts, mean terminal reward `q_s(a)`, the equal-weight mean and population standard deviation across statistical actions, and `A^state`. Also save each action's successor-state key and groups of distinct actions sharing a successor state, with sample counts. Count repeated visits separately and identify shared trajectory IDs where applicable.
- Parent gate (`parent_gate`): State ID, `K_s`, `ceil(0.2K_s)`, each visit's `A_1` and absolute value, ranking boundary, tied candidates and randomly selected visit positions, deduplicated candidate actions, parent standard deviation, and zero-variance reason. Record both selected and unselected visits.
- Child gate (`child_gate`): Parent state, candidate action, and child state IDs; all child-state visit positions; each `a'` (including observed `INVALID`) with its `n/q/A_2`; observed distinct legal and statistical action counts; child standard deviation; `σ_child/σ_parent`; separate outcomes and failure reasons for the five-action and `ω` gates; observed legal-action count `d` and `ceil(d/2)`; and absolute-advantage ordering, boundary ties, and randomly selected legal `a'` set. Retain failed candidates too.
- Advantage (`advantage`): For every visit position, baseline `A^state`, first-stage selection flag, whether its action passed the gate, whether it received `λ`, whether it is the second step in a passed chain, whether the current `a'` was selected, `β A_2`, overlap flag, final advantage `Â`, and each component of that final value. Record the fixed batch ID of old trajectories used for review.
- Update: Arm, old/current/reference policy versions, trajectory IDs, generated-token counts and mask, per-token new-to-old probability ratios, clipping fraction, per-token KL and coefficient, reductions within steps, trajectories, and batch, objective value, gradient and parameter-update statistics, optimizer-state reference, and checkpoint/configuration references. Keep sufficient information to recompute the objective; if the data are large, shard them losslessly and record checksums.
- Evaluation (`eval`): Checkpoint, test puzzle IDs, attempt count, decoding settings, full interaction and correctness for each attempt, puzzle-level success metric, invalid-action rate, generated tokens, calls, and cost. Retain per-puzzle results and use puzzles, rather than repeated trajectories of one puzzle, as independent statistical units.

Aggregate tables should also report state/action coverage distributions by layer; the fraction with `d_s≥5`; parent and child zero-variance rates; individual and joint gate pass rates; fractions of visits receiving `λ` and `β`; overlap count; actual `|A_1|` distribution among first-stage selections; `n_s(a)` distributions; each arm's training success rate and confidence interval; invalid-action rate; and total model budget. For complete two-step chains, report both the probability of entering the child state and, conditional on entry, the probabilities of selecting positive- or negative-`A_2` actions. Also report their products as complete-branch frequencies. Fix the definitions and estimation samples for "good" and "bad" branches before analysis. Distinguish observed frequencies from action probabilities parsed from the model.

These logs do not authorize a reliability gate, automatic retries, caching, early stopping, dense rewards, or training on labels from exhaustive solutions. Decide any such method change separately and apply its relevant parts consistently to both arms.
